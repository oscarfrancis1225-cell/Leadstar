# LeadStar Editorial Style Guide

All published content is insurance advertising under state law. The licensed producer is responsible for every word, including AI-assisted drafts.

## Voice

Write like a friend. Short and medium sentences. Question first, then a plain answer. A 10-year-old should understand every sentence. Adults should still want to keep reading.

Calm, approachable, professional, warm, clear, human, educational, trustworthy, and direct.

Write as if speaking with one intelligent person who wants clarity.

## Purpose

Every article exists to help a visitor understand a financial topic. Ranking is never the reason a page is created. This is an educational insurance site, not a quote mill.

## Sentences

Vary rhythm. Prefer short and medium sentences. Explain jargon or remove it. Kid-plain does not mean babyish. It means every sentence can be followed on the first read.

## Hooks

Give context first. A hook should make a reader think "this is relevant to me," not "this headline is trying to trick me." No clickbait, fake urgency, or manufactured fear.

## Stories

Use real stories only with permission. Otherwise use clearly labeled hypotheticals ("Imagine a household where..." / "Consider a hypothetical example..."). Never invent clients.

Center visitor-facing story copy on Esther Francis's real published story: she is a single parent in Boca Raton; she starts with the paycheck, not a product name.

## Story spine

Use these seven beats as the article spine for web articles. This is not a video structure. Do not add video, iframes, or short-form video features. Do not label the beats "Scene 1." Headings should be specific to the topic.

1. **Emotional hook**: first 1-2 paragraphs plus the H1. Context in the first line. Challenge a common belief. Plant a question. Open a loop; do not close it in paragraph 1.
2. **Curiosity**: the thing most people miss.
3. **Discovery**: the real mechanism. Keep sourced facts. Do not invent numbers.
4. **Mistake**: the common error. Use "but" / "therefore" logic, not "and then" plotting.
5. **Reframe**: a better way to think about the decision.
6. **Framework**: a practical checklist. Name the decision, then look at options.
7. **Payoff**: a memorable last line that leads into the conversation CTA. Make the reader want to do the decision correctly, not merely "learn more." No application on this website.

## Claims

Source before publishing. If a number cannot be verified against a primary authority, omit it.

## Advice

Educational, not individualized. Do not tell a reader what product to buy.

## CTA

Helpful next step after value. Learn, understand, talk. Do not interrupt every few paragraphs with a quote demand. Do not chase "life insurance quotes" as a promise. Figure out how much you need first; a quote comes later.

## Keywords

Natural vocabulary from the reader's question. No stuffing. Use these phrases in titles, meta descriptions, and body where they honestly fit:

- life insurance
- term life insurance
- how much life insurance do I need
- term vs whole life / term life vs whole life
- affordable term life insurance (only as a question people ask, never a cheapness claim)
- whole life insurance
- retirement income / Social Security
- turning 65 Medicare / Medicare enrollment period (not "window")
- final expense insurance
- mortgage protection
- health insurance after a job change

Do not pivot the brand to "using life insurance to build wealth."

## Length

Intent coverage determines length. There is no word-count target. Many articles will land around 1,200-2,500 words; shorter is fine when the question is fully answered.

## Tables

Use tables only for genuine comparisons. Use semantic HTML. Keep tables mobile-safe with horizontal scroll if needed.

## Banned language

peace of mind; unlock; navigate; journey; landscape; in today's world; in today's fast-paced world; em dashes (U+2014); GET A QUOTE NOW; you won't believe; secret trick; Expert Advice (as a blog label)

Visitor copy bans "right order" and "in the right order." Ban "window" and "enrollment window" except when quoting an official Medicare "enrollment period." Ban insider process-speak: "sequence," "in the right order," "protection conversations in the right order."

See also the prohibited financial-outcome terms in `content/compliance/claims-verification.md`.

## Medicare bright line

Explain program mechanics only: parts, enrollment periods, penalties as concepts, creditable coverage. Never mention specific plan benefits, premiums, cost-sharing, star ratings, or carrier names. Never compare Medicare plans. Say "enrollment period" or "the months around 65," not "window."

## Final-expense vocabulary

Do not call coverage a state-regulated benefit, government program, or burial program. Do not imply Medicare or Social Security affiliation.

## Bylines

Display: "Prepared by the LeadStar editorial team (Reviewed by Esther Francis, Financial Services Professional) [date]."

Never attach that byline unless a signed review attestation exists in `content/reviews/`.

## Thin pages

Do not create age-sliced or city-sliced doorway pages.
